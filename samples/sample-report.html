critical 1. Buttons with no readable name

1 instance(s) on 1 of 1 page(s) · rule reference · WCAG 4.1.2

Who this affects: Screen reader users hear "button" with no idea what it does (menu toggles, close icons, quantity +/−).

Where we found it
  • button
    <button><svg width="10" height="10"></svg></button>
    http://localhost:8767/bad.html

How to fix: Give icon-only buttons an aria-label and hide the decorative icon.

<button type="button" aria-label="Open menu" aria-expanded="false">
  <svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false">…</svg>
</button>

critical 2. Images have no text alternative

1 instance(s) on 1 of 1 page(s) · rule reference · WCAG 1.1.1

Who this affects: Blind customers using screen readers hear "image" or a file name instead of what the product looks like.

Where we found it
  • img[src="x.jpg"]
    <img src="x.jpg">
    http://localhost:8767/bad.html

How to fix: Give every meaningful image alt text that says what it shows ("Navy linen shirt, front view"). Give purely decorative images an empty alt="". In Shopify, set this under Products → Media → "Add alt text".

<img src="shirt.jpg" alt="Navy linen shirt, front view">
<img src="divider.svg" alt="">  <!-- decorative -->

serious 4. Text is too low-contrast to read

1 instance(s) on 1 of 1 page(s) · rule reference · WCAG 1.4.3

Who this affects: People with low vision, older customers, and anyone using a phone in sunlight.

Where we found it
  • p
    <p class="low">Low contrast text on white</p>
    http://localhost:8767/bad.html

How to fix: Body text needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 against its background, and large text (24px, or 18.7px bold) needs 3:1. Darken the text or lighten the background. Light-grey text on white and white text on pastel buttons are the usual offenders.

/* e.g. in your theme CSS */
.price--compare, .caption, .footer a { color: #595959; } /* 7:1 on white */
.button--primary { background: #1a5d38; color: #fff; }  /* 7.3:1 */

serious 8. Page scrolls sideways when zoomed

1 instance(s) on 1 of 1 page(s) · WCAG 1.4.10

Who this affects: Low-vision customers who zoom to 400% have to scroll in two directions to read each line.

Where we found it
  • document
    page is 908px wide at a 320px viewport
    http://localhost:8767/bad.html

How to fix: Find the element wider than the screen (often fixed-width tables, images or carousels) and make it fluid.

img, video, table { max-width: 100%; height: auto; }

moderate 9. Content outside page landmarks

6 instance(s) on 1 of 1 page(s) · rule reference · WCAG 1.3.1 (best practice)

Who this affects: Screen reader users jump between header, main and footer. Content outside them is easy to miss.

Where we found it
  • img[src="x.jpg"]
    <img src="x.jpg">
    http://localhost:8767/bad.html
  • h3
    <h3>Products</h3>
    http://localhost:8767/bad.html
  • p

    http://localhost:8767/bad.html

    <input type="email" placeholder="Email" style="">
    http://localhost:8767/bad.html

How to fix: Wrap the page in <header>, <main id="MainContent"> and <footer>, including announcement bars and popups.

<header>…</header>
<main id="MainContent">…</main>
<footer>…</footer>

moderate 13. No "Skip to content" link

1 instance(s) on 1 of 1 page(s) · WCAG 2.4.1

Who this affects: Keyboard users must tab through the whole header and menu on every page.

How to fix: Add a skip link as the first element in <body>. It can stay hidden until it receives focus.

<a class="skip-link" href="#MainContent">Skip to content</a>

.skip-link{position:absolute;left:-999px}.skip-link:focus{left:8px;top:8px;z-index:999;background:#fff;padding:8px 12px}

moderate 14. An accessibility overlay widget is installed

1 instance(s) on 1 of 1 page(s) · WCAG n/a

Who this affects: Disabled users often find overlays get in the way. In 2025 the US FTC fined the largest overlay vendor $1M for claiming its widget made sites compliant.

How to fix: An overlay doesn't repair the underlying code, which is what this report addresses. Many accessibility advocates recommend removing it once the code fixes are in, and it costs $490–$3,990 a year that you could stop paying.

Manual review

Example manual finding: The cart icon link has no name, so a screen reader announces only "link". In sections/header.liquid, add aria-label="Cart, {{ cart.item_count }} items" to the cart link and aria-hidden="true" to the SVG inside it.

About this report

This report combines automated testing (axe-core rules, keyboard traversal, 320px reflow, overlay detection) with a manual review of the pages listed. Automated tools find roughly a third to a half of accessibility problems, which is why the manual section matters. This is a technical assessment, not legal advice, and no audit can guarantee that a business will not receive a legal claim. Fixing the issues above meaningfully reduces barriers for disabled customers and is the strongest good-faith step a business can take.
